East Tennessee quietly holds some of the best freshwater living in the country. Three lakes do most of the work, and they're genuinely different markets.

## Fort Loudoun Lake

The in-town lake. Fort Loudoun runs from downtown Knoxville through [Louisville]({{ '/areas/louisville.html' | relative_url }}) and past [Lenoir City]({{ '/areas/lenoir-city.html' | relative_url }}) — which means you can live on the water and still be twenty minutes from a Tennessee football Saturday. Waterfront here carries the premium you'd expect; it's the luxury market's home turf.

## Tellico Lake

Cleaner, quieter, and master-planned. [Tellico Village]({{ '/areas/tellico-village.html' | relative_url }}) is the anchor — golf, marinas, clubs, and a resort pace that draws retirees and second-home buyers from everywhere. Outside the Village, Tellico shoreline through Loudon and Monroe counties offers some of the region's best value-per-waterfront-foot.

## Watts Bar Lake

The sleeper. Southwest of Knoxville through Roane and Meigs counties ([Decatur]({{ '/areas/decatur.html' | relative_url }}) side included), Watts Bar is huge, uncrowded, and the most affordable serious waterfront in East Tennessee. If the dream is a dock and acreage without a seven-figure price, this is where I take people.

## What lake buyers always learn the hard way

Lakefront is a different purchase, and this is where my contractor's license earns its keep:

- **TVA owns the shoreline rules.** Dock permits, shoreline alterations, and water-use rights run through TVA — never assume an existing dock is permitted or transferable.
- **Seawalls and rip-rap are five-figure line items.** I inspect them on every lake showing.
- **Water depth is seasonal.** Winter drawdown can turn "lakefront" into "mud-front" on the wrong lot. I check drawdown maps before you fall in love.
- **Septic, wells, and slopes** — lake lots concentrate every rural-property question into one parcel.
