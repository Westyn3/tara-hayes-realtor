I hold two Tennessee licenses — real estate broker and building contractor. The second one changes how I walk every house, and it's saved my buyers more money than any negotiation trick I know.

Here's what I'm actually looking at while you're looking at the kitchen.

## Outside, before we even go in

- **The roofline.** Sagging ridges mean structural movement or decking problems. Shingle age I can read from the driveway.
- **Grading and drainage.** East Tennessee sits on clay and karst. Water running *toward* a foundation is the start of half the crawlspace problems in Knox County.
- **Brick cracks.** Hairline stair-stepping in mortar is usually settling; wide or displaced cracks are a conversation.

## Inside, past the staging

- **Floors that slope toward the middle** — posts and girders in the crawlspace.
- **Doors that don't latch** — frames racking as the house moves.
- **The electrical panel brand.** A couple of specific 1970s–80s panels are insurance problems and replacement line items. I check every one.
- **HVAC age vs. listing claims.** The data plate doesn't lie; listings sometimes round generously.

## In the crawlspace (yes, I actually go in)

Knoxville is crawlspace country, and it's where the real information lives: moisture, past repairs, amateur wiring, insulation falling off the joists, and the quality of every renovation the listing bragged about. Ten minutes under a house tells me more than an hour inside it.

## What this means for your offer

None of this replaces a licensed home inspection — you should always get one. What it does is change *when* you learn things. My buyers know the big-ticket picture **before** they write the offer, so we price it in from the start instead of renegotiating in a panic ten days later. And when the inspection report comes back, I know what each finding actually costs to fix — so we ask for the right credits and skip the theater.
