If Knoxville has a neighborhood that sells itself over dinner, it's [Bearden]({{ '/areas/bearden.html' | relative_url }}). Ten minutes to downtown, five to West Town, and an evening's worth of restaurants, galleries, and shops along Kingston Pike — with the greenway stitching it all together.

## Why it holds its value

Bearden is the rare Knoxville market that appeals to nearly everyone at once: young professionals who want walkability, families zoning for Sequoyah and Bearden schools, downsizers leaving big West Knox lots, and UT-adjacent buyers who want midtown without the student churn. When every buyer pool wants the same streets, values don't wobble much. Inventory is the constraint — these homes change hands quietly and fast.

## What the streets look like

The housing stock is wonderfully varied: 1940s–60s cottages and ranches off Sutherland and Northshore, stately homes toward [Sequoyah Hills]({{ '/blog/sequoyah-hills-knoxville-guide/' | relative_url }}), condos and townhomes along the Pike for the lock-and-leave crowd, and infill new construction tucked onto lots that used to hold one small house and now hold two nice ones.

## The contractor's note

Midtown charm comes with midtown bones. On Bearden's older cottages I'm checking the same list every time: panel and wiring generation, cast iron versus updated drains, foundation settling on these clay lots, and the quality of whatever flip work happened between the last owner and you.
