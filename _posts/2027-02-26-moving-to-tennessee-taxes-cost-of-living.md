Half my [relocation clients]({{ '/relocation-to-knoxville.html' | relative_url }}) mention the same thing in the first phone call: no state income tax. It's true, and it's significant — but it's the start of the math, not the whole equation. Here's the honest version I give every family moving to Knoxville.

## What you actually save

Tennessee has no state income tax on wages — for a household coming from a 5–9% state, that's real, recurring money. Property taxes are the quieter win: Knox County's effective rates run well below what most arrivals from the Northeast, Midwest, or Texas are used to paying, and it compounds every year you own. Utilities and insurance tend to land at or below national averages, and day-to-day costs — fuel, services, dining — still feel gentle to most transplants.

## What to budget honestly

Sales tax is the trade: Tennessee's combined rate is among the nation's highest, and it includes groceries. Home prices in Knoxville have climbed with our popularity — buyers from California or the DC area still find us refreshing, while buyers from rural markets find us real. And if you're keeping income in another state — a rental property, a business — that state may still tax it.

## The move itself

Where you land matters more than the averages. The same budget behaves very differently in [Farragut]({{ '/areas/farragut.html' | relative_url }}), [Maryville]({{ '/areas/maryville.html' | relative_url }}), [Oak Ridge]({{ '/areas/oak-ridge.html' | relative_url }}), or out by the lakes — different taxes, commutes, and school patterns. That's the conversation worth having before the moving truck is booked.
