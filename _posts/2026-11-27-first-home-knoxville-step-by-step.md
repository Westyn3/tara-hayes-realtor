I love first-time buyers. You ask the best questions, you care the most, and a year later you send me photos of what you did with the backyard. Here's the roadmap I walk every first-timer through.

## Step 1: Talk to a lender before you scroll

Pre-approval first. It costs nothing, takes a day, and turns "window shopping" into "shopping." It also surfaces the surprises — a credit ding, a student-loan calculation — while there's still time to fix them. Don't have a lender? I'll connect you with the local ones my clients consistently thank me for.

## Step 2: Learn your real monthly number

The sticker price isn't the payment. Principal, interest, taxes, insurance, maybe HOA — I make every first-time buyer a real monthly number for every house we tour. Knox County's property taxes are gentle by national standards, which is part of why your money goes further here.

## Step 3: Pick your lane

In 2026, realistic first-home lanes in the Knoxville area look like: condos and townhomes from the $200s; solid starter homes in [Powell]({{ '/areas/powell.html' | relative_url }}), [Halls]({{ '/areas/halls.html' | relative_url }}), [Karns]({{ '/areas/karns.html' | relative_url }}), and [South Knoxville]({{ '/areas/south-knoxville.html' | relative_url }}) in the $300s; newer construction in [Hardin Valley]({{ '/areas/hardin-valley.html' | relative_url }}) and [Maryville]({{ '/areas/maryville.html' | relative_url }}) from the $400s.

## Step 4: Tour with someone who sees through walls

This is where I'm different, and I'll say it plainly: I'm a licensed building contractor. On your first purchase, the most expensive mistake isn't overpaying by 2% — it's buying the wrong house entirely. I'll tell you which "needs a little love" listing is a smart project and which is a money pit wearing new paint.

## Step 5: Offer, inspect, close

We write a competitive-but-protected offer, you get a professional inspection (always), we negotiate repairs from real repair costs, and thirty-ish days later you're holding keys.
