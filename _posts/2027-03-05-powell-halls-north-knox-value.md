While everyone bids against each other out west, North Knox County keeps quietly closing deals. [Powell]({{ '/areas/powell.html' | relative_url }}) and [Halls]({{ '/areas/halls.html' | relative_url }}) deliver what first-time buyers and young families keep telling me they want — a real house, a real yard, a reasonable payment — without the West Knox premium.

## What north money buys

The same budget that gets a dated condo near Kingston Pike gets a three-bedroom with a yard in Powell, and often newer construction in Halls. Emory Road and the I-75 corridor keep pulling new builds north, and the dollars-per-square-foot gap against [Farragut and Hardin Valley]({{ '/blog/farragut-vs-hardin-valley/' | relative_url }}) remains one of the widest honest spreads in the county.

## Life up north

Powell has its own gravity — the high school, Emory Road's restaurant row filling in, and quick I-75 access that puts downtown twenty minutes away. Halls runs a little more rural at the edges, with bigger lots and Norris Lake day trips in easy reach. Both feed growing school communities, and both still feel like places where neighbors know each other, because they do.

## The contractor's note

North Knox stock splits between 1970s–90s established neighborhoods and the new construction wave. On the established side I'm checking roof and HVAC generations, settling, and the DIY quality of forty years of owner projects. On new builds, don't let a builder's shine skip the inspection — fast framing crews make fast mistakes, and I've found them at every price point.
