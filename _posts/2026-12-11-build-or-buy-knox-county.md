I'm licensed as both a broker and a building contractor, which means I get this question more than any other: *should we just build?* Here's the math I walk families through.

## What building really costs in 2026

Around Knox County, quality custom construction generally runs $200–$300+ per square foot before land — more for complex sites and high-end finishes. Add the lot ($80K–$250K+ depending on where), site work (clearing, grading, septic or sewer tap, utilities — routinely $40K–$100K on raw land), and carrying costs while you build. A "we'll just build a 2,400 square foot house" conversation is usually a $700K–$900K reality by the time it has a driveway.

## What building buys you

Everything where you want it. No inherited problems. Today's codes, today's efficiency, a floor plan that fits your actual family. And in [Hardin Valley]({{ '/areas/hardin-valley.html' | relative_url }}), [Powell]({{ '/areas/powell.html' | relative_url }}), and [Maryville]({{ '/areas/maryville.html' | relative_url }}), production builders deliver it faster and cheaper than custom — with trade-offs in lot size and finish choices.

## What buying existing buys you

Mature trees, established neighborhoods, known schools, and — critically in 2026 — a price per square foot that new construction can't match in most of the county. The [Farragut]({{ '/areas/farragut.html' | relative_url }}) brick traditional at $240/sqft would cost $320+ to build today on the same lot, if you could find the lot.

## The contractor's tiebreakers

- **Building:** only as good as its supervision. If you build, your agent should be reading the plans, walking the pre-drywall stage, and holding the punch list. (This is, not coincidentally, what I do.)
- **Buying existing:** the inspection is everything. A well-maintained 1998 house is a better buy than a carelessly-built 2024 one — and I can tell you which is which from the crawlspace.
- **The sleeper option:** buying a lot now and building in a year or two. [Land]({{ '/land-lots.html' | relative_url }}) is the step people skip thinking about; I evaluate buildability before you buy.
