Every June, someone calls me from a rented pontoon wanting to buy on the lake "this summer." I help them — but they're shopping at the year's peak, against every other family who had the same sunny-day idea. The smart lake money moves in March and April, before the water warms and the premiums arrive.

## Why spring wins on the water

[Lakefront]({{ '/luxury-lakefront.html' | relative_url }}) inventory rises ahead of the season as owners list for summer — but the buyer wave lags until school's nearly out. In that gap, you're choosing from fresh listings with less competition, and sellers who've carried a lake home through winter are often readier to deal in March than they will be on Memorial Day. You close in time for your first full summer instead of your first fall.

## Inspect the water as hard as the house

Waterfront is the one market where the lot can outweigh the structure. Dock permits and TVA shoreline rules, seawall condition, water depth at your dock in winter pool — [Fort Loudoun, Tellico, and Watts Bar]({{ '/blog/lakefront-living-east-tennessee/' | relative_url }}) each behave differently when TVA draws the lakes down. A gorgeous summer listing photo tells you nothing about February water. I do.

## The contractor's note

Lake homes work harder than city homes: moisture, foundation movement on sloped shorelines, decks and docks aging in the weather, septic systems doing heavy summer duty. As a licensed building contractor, I read the seawall, the crawlspace, and the dock's bones before you fall for the sunset. The sunset's free. The seawall isn't.
