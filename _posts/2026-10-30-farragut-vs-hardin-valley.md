If I had a dollar for every family that asked me "Farragut or Hardin Valley?", I could buy a lot in either one. They're the two strongest family markets in West Knox County, and they're more different than the map suggests.

## The case for Farragut

[Farragut]({{ '/areas/farragut.html' | relative_url }}) is the established choice — an incorporated town with its own parks, greenways, and famously well-kept subdivisions. The schools are the headline: Farragut's feeder pattern is the most requested in the county, and it shows up in resale values. Homes here are mostly 1990s–2010s, brick, mature trees, sidewalks. You're buying a finished neighborhood.

The trade-off: inventory is tight, lots are settled, and you'll compete for the good ones. Expect the $500s to $900s for most established homes, with luxury streets well past that.

## The case for Hardin Valley

[Hardin Valley]({{ '/areas/hardin-valley.html' | relative_url }}) is where Knoxville is growing. Twenty years ago it was farmland; today it's subdivision after subdivision of new and nearly-new construction feeding Hardin Valley Academy. Young families dominate. Your money buys newer square footage — the $400s–$600s gets you what the same spend won't in Farragut.

The trade-offs are growth's usual ones: construction traffic, schools managing enrollment, and newer landscaping that needs a decade to catch up to Farragut's canopy.
