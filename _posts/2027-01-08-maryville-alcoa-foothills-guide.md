Ask ten East Tennesseans where they'd move if they left Knoxville, and half will say [Maryville]({{ '/areas/maryville.html' | relative_url }}). Blount County's twin cities sit twenty minutes south of town with the Smokies filling the windshield the whole way down.

## What draws people here

Three things, consistently. The schools — Maryville City Schools has been one of Tennessee's top districts for decades, and families pay a premium to be in it. The views — this is genuine foothills country, and plenty of ordinary neighborhoods come with un-ordinary mountain backdrops. And the pace — downtown Maryville has a courthouse-square feel with restaurants and a greenbelt, while Alcoa brings the airport, Springbrook's historic streets, and some of the area's newest retail.

## What your money buys

Maryville city limits trade at a premium for the school district — established brick homes from the $400s, newer construction from the $500s. Alcoa and the county areas run gentler, and the farther you push toward Townsend and the mountains, the more land comes with the house. Acreage with a view is still findable here in a way West Knoxville lost years ago.

## The commute question

McGhee Tyson workers, DENSO and the Alcoa industrial corridor, and remote workers barely think about it. Downtown Knoxville commuters should drive Alcoa Highway at 8 a.m. once before committing — it's improved with the new construction, but it's honest to call it the one trade-off.

## The contractor's note

Blount County housing stock skews two directions: mid-century homes near the old Alcoa plants, and post-2000 subdivisions. On the older stock I'm checking foundations, galvanized plumbing, and additions; on the newer, grading and drainage — foothill lots move water, and the cheap ones move it toward the house.
