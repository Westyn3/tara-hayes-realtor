Every city has the neighborhood. In Knoxville, it's [Sequoyah Hills]({{ '/areas/sequoyah-hills.html' | relative_url }}) — the 1920s streetcar suburb laid out along Cherokee Boulevard, where dogwood-lined medians run down to the Tennessee River and the houses have names, not just numbers.

## Why buyers fall for it

Start with the boulevard itself. Cherokee Boulevard's grassy median is essentially a two-mile linear park — walkers, runners, and strollers use it all day, and Talahi Park and Sequoyah Park put the riverfront a short walk from most streets. You're ten minutes from downtown and five from Bearden's restaurants, yet the neighborhood feels like it was designed in a quieter century, because it was.

The housing stock is the other half of the romance: Tudor revivals, Georgians, Colonials, and low-slung midcentury ranches on the quieter streets — most built between the 1920s and 1960s, many renovated beautifully, a few still waiting for the right owner to do it.

## What it costs

Sequoyah Hills trades like the landmark it is. Renovated homes on the boulevard and the river side routinely run seven figures, and the neighborhood anchors much of [Knoxville's luxury market]({{ '/luxury-lakefront.html' | relative_url }}). But the interior streets still offer entry points in the high six figures — smaller footprints, same address, same walk to the river. Inventory is thin in every season; the right house here is bought by the buyer who was ready before it listed.

## The contractor's note

This is where my second license earns its keep. A 1928 Tudor is a different animal from new construction: plaster walls, original windows, clay sewer laterals, additions stacked on additions across a century of owners. When I walk a Sequoyah Hills home, I'm reading the foundation, the roofline, the electrical panel, and the quality of every renovation layer — because in this neighborhood, you're not just buying a house, you're inheriting ninety years of decisions. Some of those decisions were excellent. I'll tell you which ones weren't before you sign.

## Is it your neighborhood?

If you want new, open-concept, and maintenance-free, Sequoyah Hills will frustrate you — look at [Farragut or Hardin Valley]({{ '/blog/farragut-vs-hardin-valley/' | relative_url }}) instead. But if you want architecture, canopy, river light, and an address that has meant something in Knoxville for a hundred years, there's nothing else like it.
