Every December, someone tells me they'll "wait until spring to list." Sometimes that's right. But after eleven winters of Knoxville closings, I can tell you the conventional wisdom costs some sellers real money.

## Winter buyers are serious buyers

Nobody tours houses on a 38-degree Saturday for fun. The looky-loos hibernate; what's left are relocating families on corporate timelines, buyers who just sold and need to land, and people whose life changed in a way that can't wait for April. Fewer showings, far higher intent.

## Your competition is on vacation

Knoxville inventory drops hard from Thanksgiving to February — exactly when relocation season (new year, new job) sends motivated buyers into the market. The supply-demand math quietly favors winter sellers: your home isn't one of forty new listings this week; it's one of six.

## Homes show beautifully in winter — if prepared

A warm, well-lit home on a gray day sells *feeling* as much as footage. The prep list is different, though, and my contractor's side gets opinionated here: service the HVAC before listing (every buyer flips the thermostat), protect the showing path from mud, keep gutters clear so melt doesn't paint foundation stains, and light every dark corner — winter showings happen at 5:30pm in the dark.

## The honest counterpoint

If your home's best feature is the yard, the pool, or mature landscaping, spring photography is worth waiting for. And luxury listings often benefit from the spring buyer pool. This isn't one-size-fits-all — it's a pricing-and-positioning decision, which is exactly what a listing consultation is for.
