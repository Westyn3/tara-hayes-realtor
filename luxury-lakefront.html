Waterfront real estate near Knoxville

Knoxville sits in the middle of the Tennessee Valley's lake country, and each lake lives differently. Fort Loudoun Lake runs right through Knoxville and out past Farragut and Lenoir City, with in-town waterfront and long river views. Tellico Lake draws buyers to Tellico Village and the quieter coves around Loudon and Vonore. Melton Hill is a narrower, calmer lake near Oak Ridge and Hardin Valley, and Norris Lake to the north is known for clear water and a more remote, getaway feel.

What makes lake property different

  • Dock and shoreline permits. On TVA lakes, docks and shoreline work are permitted through TVA. Tara checks what's permitted on a lot, and whether the dock on the water matches the paperwork, before you make an offer.
  • Water levels. TVA lowers lake levels in winter, and some lakes, like Norris, drop much further than others. A lot that looks perfect in July can sit far from the water in February, so it pays to know the seasonal depth at the dock.
  • Seawalls and erosion. The condition of a seawall or riprap shoreline can be one of the most expensive surprises on a lake home.
  • Septic, wells and slope. Many lake lots are outside city sewer and sit on steep terrain. A contractor's eye on the septic system, drainage and access matters here.
  • Systems built for the water. Boat lifts, dock electrical, geothermal and whole-home generators all deserve a closer look than a standard inspection gives them.
