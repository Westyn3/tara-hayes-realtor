Sequoyah Hills real estate at a glance

Sequoyah Hills is the 1920s streetcar suburb laid out along Cherokee Boulevard on Knoxville's west side, between the Tennessee River and Kingston Pike. The boulevard's grassy, dogwood-lined median works like a two-mile park, and Talahi Park and Sequoyah Park put the riverfront within a short walk of most streets. Downtown is about ten minutes away and Bearden's restaurants and shops about five.

What the homes are like

Most homes here were built between the 1920s and the 1960s: Tudor revivals, Georgians and Colonials on the boulevard and the streets near the river, and low-slung midcentury ranches on the quieter interior streets. Many have been renovated beautifully; a few are still waiting for the right owner to do it. Lots are mature and tree-covered, and no two blocks look quite alike.

What it costs

Renovated homes on Cherokee Boulevard and the river side routinely run seven figures, and the neighborhood anchors much of Knoxville's luxury market. The interior streets still offer entry points in the high six figures: smaller footprints, the same address, the same walk to the river. Inventory is thin in every season, so the buyers who get the right house are usually the ones who were ready before it listed. Buying an older home here: the contractor's checklist

A 1928 Tudor is a different animal from new construction. Before you make an offer, Tara looks at the things that matter most in a home this age:

  • Foundation, drainage and grading around the house
  • Roof, chimneys, gutters and the condition of original windows
  • Electrical panel and wiring, including any older circuits still in use
  • Plumbing and the sewer line from the house to the street
  • Plaster walls and ceilings, and how well past additions tie into the original house
  • The quality of each renovation layer, and whether permits were pulled

You're not just buying a house in Sequoyah Hills; you're inheriting decades of decisions by past owners. Tara will tell you which ones were good and which ones weren't before you sign.
